Meet the founder

Meet Leeza Mathew.

Leeza Mathew is a multifaceted artist and technologist who brings a storyteller’s heart and an engineer’s discipline to everything she creates. She is the founder and artistic director of Upasana Dance Academy in the Greater Seattle area.

Her artistic journey began with Bharatanatyam, and she continues her training under Guru Suchitra Vishweswaran. Those deep classical roots are the foundation of her choreography: original works that honor tradition while speaking to contemporary audiences.

Leeza’s storytelling reaches well beyond the dance stage. She wrote and directed the acclaimed play Mudranguleeyam (2019). Her short film Caged was selected for festivals including the Mumbai International Film Festival, organized by India’s Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, and was released on Cue Channel. She also wrote and directed the musical Navarasamaala, released by Zee Music.

Alongside her art, Leeza has built a two-decade career in enterprise technology, including senior engineering and program leadership at Microsoft. Across dance, theatre, film and technology, one mission connects her work: to tell stories that resonate across cultures and generations.

Solo classical dance series

Samarpana Series

Samarpana is Upasana’s solo Indian classical dance series. The Seattle area is home to a remarkable number of gifted classical dancers. What they often lack is a stage, and an audience to meet them there. Samarpana exists to change that.

Our mission is twofold: to cultivate and nurture a growing audience for Indian classical dance, and to give solo performers, from established schools and independent practice alike, a dedicated stage of their own.

Supported by 4Culture

The Samarpana Series has received a grant from 4Culture, King County’s cultural funding agency. This support helps us bring solo classical dance to more stages and more audiences, including our recent performance at the Redmond Senior Center.

From Seattle to the stages of South India.

2026

Samam

In 2026, Upasana presented Samam, an original dance production, at some of Kerala’s most prestigious venues: the Poornathrayeesa Sangeetha Sabha, the Soorya Festival and Changampuzha Park. Performing on these historic stages, before audiences steeped in the classical arts, was a proud moment for our dancers and a homecoming for the tradition we teach.

2024

Nrithaayana

Nrithaayana was Upasana’s South Indian dance tour, led by Leeza Mathew with her students. The journey took our dancers to Chidambaram, the temple town of Nataraja and a spiritual home of Bharatanatyam, and on to Coimbatore, Kozhikode and Thrippunithura. For our students, it was a chance to dance where the art form was born.

From the Founder’s Desk

“Bharatanatyam gave me a language for stories I could not tell any other way.”

Dear friends,

When I started Upasana, I wanted a place where dancers could find their own creative voice inside a tradition that is centuries old. Bharatanatyam gave me a language for stories I could not tell any other way, and I wanted to share that gift.

Over the years, Upasana has grown into more than a school. It is a stage for new work, a home for solo artists through Samarpana, and a way for our community to give back. Every performance we present carries a purpose beyond the applause.

Thank you for being part of this journey. I hope to see you in class, in the audience, or on stage.

With gratitude,

Leeza Mathew
Founder & Artistic Director

Bhishma – The Eternal Vow

Upasana’s next major production turns to one of the most towering figures of the Mahabharata. Bhishma brings to life the stories surrounding his extraordinary life, told through Theatre.

Born Devavrata, son of King Shantanu and the river goddess Ganga, he gave up a throne and took a vow of lifelong celibacy so that his father could marry again. That unbreakable oath earned him the name Bhishma, and the boon of choosing the hour of his own death. Bound by his word, he served the throne of Hastinapura through generations, and finally fought for the Kauravas at Kurukshetra against the Pandavas he loved.

Through expressive dance, music and storytelling, Bhishma explores duty, sacrifice and the cost of a promise kept at any price, a story that still speaks to us thousands of years later.

Supported by 4Culture: Bhishma has been awarded a grant from 4Culture, King County’s cultural funding agency.
